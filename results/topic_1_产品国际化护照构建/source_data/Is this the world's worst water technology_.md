This month in Global Water Intelligence my colleague [Tom Gould](https://www.linkedin.com/in/thomas-gould-b5b968150/) has [a story](https://www.globalwaterintel.com/global-water-intelligence-magazine/21/5/general/selling-water-at-150-m3-to-the-world-s-poorest-people-with-billionaire-backing) on [Zero Mass Water](https://www.zeromasswater.com/), an Arizona-based water technology start up which has raised more than $50 million to produce solar powered atmospheric water generators. This is a summary of why it is so bad:

![No alt text provided for this image](https://:0)

No one at Zero Mass Water would talk to us for the story. There have been the subject of some spectacular take downs ( [this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vc7WqVMCABg&feature=youtu.be&a=) is probably the best), but these are lost among the acres of admiring reviews from people who don't understand how inefficient the technology is. To its shame MIT gave the company a $500,000 [prize](https://lemelson.mit.edu/prize) last year.

In many ways there is nothing wrong with what Zero Mass Water is doing. The end-users are largely happy because the system works in its own small way, and generally the panels have been paid for by donors. Some of these donors, especially development finance institutions like the Asian Development Bank, US AID, and the Inter American Development Bank ought to know better. There is no place on earth where a hydropanel is the most cost effective source of water.

But that is not the point. Zero Mass Water is not really in the business of selling water. In this crazy world of guilt-ridden billionaires, extreme poverty and anxiety about climate change and the environment, its main product is emotional. It is the feeling of being able to do faultless good in a critical world. It is cheap at the price.
